# Production recipe families

Use the numbered source catalog in `docs/research/100-producer-effects-video-outline.md` for the exact recipe and cited result. This reference selects the Crump construction family.

## Time, pitch, and traversal

Examples: Halftime (1), RevCrush (6), Reverse Stutters (18), Vocal Rewind (29), Slowed & Reverb (60), Tape Stop (61), Rev Pitch (69), Slow Down (78), Low & Slow (85), and Trip (89).

Construction choices:

- linked rate/pitch playback for tape or vinyl laws;
- independent prepared stretch and pitch/formant transforms;
- reverse traversal;
- routed pitch/formant processor for live Audio;
- deliberate transient, tonal, texture, or artifact warp mode; and
- exact-duration bounce/resampling when the result becomes source material.

## Vocal transformation

Examples: Talking Synth (8), Glitched Vocals (19), Delayed Vox (20), Whispers (24), Demon Time (26), Grainy Vocals (27), Pretty Vocal (28), Vocal Pad (30), Bouncy Vocoder (34), and Voice Gate (88).

Typical chain:

1. edit/review phrase boundaries;
2. choose pitch and formant policy;
3. choose vocoder, granular, spectral, or gate transformation;
4. preserve an intelligible dry/parallel path where needed;
5. add filtered delay/reverb; and
6. level-match at phrase and Song scale.

## Delay, ambience, and tails

Examples: Haunting Melodies (2), Ambitextures (3), Reverse Reverb (22), Reverse Delays (38), Reverb Swells (42), Double Delays (44), Delay Swells (45), Ghostly (46), Blur (47), Split Delays (48), Nice Delay (52), Tails (54), and Ping Pong Ball (99).

Choose shared Returns for continuing state. Filter feedback/wet paths, automate throws, protect the next section onset, and bounce only when reversing or replaying the wet tail as material.

## Glitch, stutter, and degradation

Examples: Distortion Fun (10), Bouncy Stutters (23), Alien Drums (37), Grainy (40), Slow Stutters (43), Open Stutter (51), Glitches (56), Broken Game (58), Crush Transition (64), Vocal Repeat (91), Ableton EZ Stuts (95), and Evolving Tremelo (98).

Choose among explicit Event/Clip repetitions, Stutter/Beat Repeat, seeded fragment selection, Crusher/Decimator, nonlinear chains, sample offset, grain processing, and rhythmic Automation. Preserve exact boundaries and deterministic replay.

## Synthesis, layering, and playable effects

Examples: Bass Booster (4), Layers (5), Simples (7), Big Bass (9), LFO Synth (12), Melody Trip (15), Bass Drop (17), Haunting Pad (25), Pad Kick (32), Kick 2 808 (36), Video Game (49), LFO Vibes (62), Specter (63), and Lead Bounce (82).

Decide whether the effect belongs in:

- the Instrument voice graph;
- a shared post-voice graph;
- a sampler/multisample/granular/spectral source;
- a transparent Effect Module; or
- a generated asset mapped into a new Instrument.

Expose a small Faceplate and test velocity, register, note lifecycle, polyphony, and repeated deterministic render.

## Transitions and section motion

Examples: Intro Suspense (65), Build Up (66), Shifty Transition (67), DJ (68), Sweepy (71), EZ Risers (72), Subby Transition (73), Cut Transition (74), Rev Transition (75), Cut & Move (76), Tran Flange (77), One At A Time (79), and Get Gated (80).

Anchor every transition to Locators. Combine no more than a few clear dimensions: density, register, bandwidth, pitch, space, repetition, and low-end presence. Check the section before, boundary, section after, and complete tail.

## Sampling, chops, and resampling

Examples: Chops (11), 1234 (13), Vibe (14), Foley Fun (81), Oneshot Bonanza (83), ONA (86), Re-Resampled (87), Drum Extraction (92), Granulator 3 (93), EZ Chops (96–97), and Sample Offset (100).

Use exact source frames, reviewed slice/transient boundaries, stable asset identity, explicit loop/direction/offset law, deterministic mappings, and atomic provenance for every generated derivative.

## Arrangement and generative material

Examples: EZ Songstarter (16), Hat Roll (35), Movements (59), Counter Melody (90), and Melody Repeater (94).

Use readable Patterns, lineage-preserving Variations, explicit Grooves, deterministic seeded generation, and reviewed Events. The generated result must remain inspectable and editable.

## Tonal, spatial, and character processing

Examples: Elegant (21), Snare Slaps (33), Slappy Chorus (39), Aesthetic Slaps (41), Flanger Delays (50), Disenchanted (53), Telephone (55), Radio (57), Club Toilet (84), and Melody/texture variants not covered above.

Build the audible result from explicit EQ/filtering, dynamics, saturation, modulation delay, room/convolution character, stereo policy, and routing. Judge at matched level in the complete mix.
