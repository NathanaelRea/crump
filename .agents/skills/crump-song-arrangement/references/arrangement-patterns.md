# Arrangement patterns

## Intro → statement → contrast → return → outro

- Intro establishes palette and pulse without full low-end density.
- Statement presents motif, bass, and groove clearly.
- Contrast changes register, rhythm, harmony, or texture while retaining identity.
- Return restores the primary idea with earned expansion.
- Outro removes roles deliberately or transforms the motif into a closing texture.

## Tension-and-release build

1. Remove low-frequency certainty.
2. Increase event density or subdivision.
3. Raise pitch, cutoff, space, or tempo with one readable curve.
4. Shorten gaps or increase repetition.
5. Leave a final breath/cut when the drop needs contrast.
6. Restore low end and primary transient hierarchy at the release.

## Drop reveal

- Filter or mute bass before the boundary.
- Reduce competing ambience and long tails.
- Place transition energy before, not over, the first decisive transient.
- Restore bandwidth, sub, width, and rhythmic completeness together only when intended.

## Call and response

- Derive the response from motif rhythm, contour, or timbre.
- Offset register, panning, source, or effect treatment.
- Leave enough negative space to hear the relationship.
- Use a Variation when lineage matters; use a new Pattern when the response makes independent decisions.

## Evolving loop

- Preserve one recognizable anchor.
- Rotate one variation family per phrase: rhythm, notes, timbre, space, or fill.
- Use deterministic probability for micro-variation.
- Reserve larger register/density changes for section boundaries.

## Breakdown reconstruction

- Begin with one harmonic or vocal identity.
- Reintroduce pulse, percussion, bass implication, and full bass in stages.
- Use resampled tails, reverse material, or filtered fragments as continuity.
- Avoid rebuilding every Track at the same boundary.

## Transition audit

For every boundary check:

- source and destination energy;
- low-end continuity or deliberate absence;
- tail collisions;
- pitch/key relationship;
- transient masking;
- automation continuity;
- exact Locator/range alignment; and
- whether the transition remains useful at matched loudness.
