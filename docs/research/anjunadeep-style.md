# Anjunadeep-style melodic house: production conventions

This research is informative and does not define Crump behavior. It supports a future fixture track and style checklist.

**Accessed:** 2026-11-25 to 2026-11-26
**Artists in scope:** Ben Böhmer, Nox Vahn, Marsh, Romain Garcia, Durante

## Evidence labels

Every claim carries one of these tags:

- **[A] Artist.** The named artist said it, in an interview or in an artist-led breakdown.
- **[P] Publication.** A production publication or tutorial says it about the genre or a track. Tutorials sold by course vendors and SEO guides are marked **[P-weak]**.
- **[I] Inference.** My own general genre convention or reading. It needs listening or measurement before it is treated as fact.

No melodies, specific-song chord charts or lyrics are reproduced here.

## Summary

- Böhmer calls his sound "Melodic, dramatic, melancholic but happy; between deep house and melodic techno" ([EDM.com](https://edm.com/interviews/ben-bohmer-hot-air-balloon-performance-interview/)) [A]. That phrase is the clearest artist-owned description of the target mood.
- The best-sourced craft techniques are all from Böhmer:
  - a resampled arp as the track's foundation;
  - a simple lead with a few notes altered every 4 bars;
  - call-and-response lines made by reprocessing the lead's own sound;
  - pads that fill the space;
  - tension built through automation rather than new parts ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)) [A].
- Across the five artists, the sound sources are consistently analog or analog-modelled: u-he Diva, Sylenth1, Roland SH-101/SH-2/ProMars, UDO Super 6, Moog Grandmother and Arturia Mini V ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/the-last-5-or-10-per-cent-drove-me-crazy-at-one-point-i-had-about-130-mixdowns-ive-come-to-realise-that-the-perfect-mix-doesnt-exist-ben-bohmer-on-overcoming-perfectionism-in-production), [Magnetic: Marsh](https://magneticmag.com/2020/11/how-it-was-made-marsh-lailonie/), [Magnetic: Durante](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/)) [A].
- The artists give no numbers for mixing or loudness. All dB, LU and ms targets below are [I] or come from platform specifications.

## 1. Composition

### Artist-sourced [A]

- **Harmony.** Böhmer uses "jazz-style chord and scale knowledge" to decide which chords work together, and often starts from piano improvisation or a "silhouette" of a song that suggests a mood ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/the-last-5-or-10-per-cent-drove-me-crazy-at-one-point-i-had-about-130-mixdowns-ive-come-to-realise-that-the-perfect-mix-doesnt-exist-ben-bohmer-on-overcoming-perfectionism-in-production)).
- **Hook construction.** Keep the melody simple. Slightly alter a few notes every 4 bars so phrases stay connected but evolve ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)).
- **Call and response.** A second synth line answers the lead in the gaps between its phrases. Böhmer calls this "Call-Response" or "Question-Answer". The answer is made by reprocessing the original sound (Soundtoys Crystallizer), which keeps the parts coherent ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)).
- **Arp as the foundation.** In "Beyond Beliefs", the arp is resampled from a fragment of Böhmer's own earlier track. Its rhythm and texture set the groove and mood for everything else ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)).
- **Interlocking parts.** Durante takes "conversation" between instruments from Duke Ellington's band: elements follow each other to form the chord progression ([DJ Life](https://djlifemag.com/2024/03/durante-enter-the-debut-interview/)).
- **Avoiding repetition.** Durante chops and reverses parts of the chord phrase so it doesn't sound repetitive. He set the remix key from the vocal ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/)).
- **Emotion.** Marsh says trance-derived emotion works "through chord progressions and melodies", alongside "a good baseline" [sic] ([Stereofox](https://stereofox.com/interviews/interview-marsh)).

### Publication [P-weak]

- **Chords.** An EDMProd guide that uses "Beyond Beliefs" as its reference voices chords as inversions held over long spans (one pad chord across 16 bars) and nudges them off the grid ([EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-melodic-house/)).
- **Rising bass.** Production Music Live suggests an uplifting lift from bass notes that step upward ([PML search listing](https://productionmusiclive.com/blogs/news/producer-notes-tinlicker-above-beyond-melodic-vocal-house-style-anjunadeep-in-ableton)).

### Inference [I]

- **Key and mode.** Minor keys and modes (Aeolian or Dorian) dominate, with major-key lifts carrying the "happy" half of "melancholic but happy". No source here gives a key census, so verify against Beatport metadata.
- **Harmonic rhythm.** Harmonic rhythm is slow: one chord per 1–2 bars, in loops of 4 or 8 bars. Extended chords (7ths, 9ths, sus2) are common, consistent with Böhmer's "jazz" remark.
- **Melodic contour.** Melodies are narrow-range, mostly stepwise and pentatonic-leaning. Tension comes from timbre, filters and automation more than from harmonic surprise, which matches Böhmer's automation-led tension.

## 2. Arrangement

### Artist-sourced [A]

- **Böhmer's "Beyond Beliefs" masterclass**, as summarised by [WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-masterclass/):
  - The structure is intro, middle and outro, with near-identical intro and outro.
  - Elements enter gradually, each with a purpose.
  - The middle is a breakdown and a build into a drop.
  - Everything comes together toward the end.
- **Mixing while arranging.** Böhmer says "80-90% of the song is already mixed" by the time the arrangement is done (same source).
- **Track order.** Böhmer tests track order using private SoundCloud playlists ([SoundCloud Sound Advice](https://soundcloud.com/stories/post/sound-advice-ben-bohmer)).
- **Testing in clubs.**
  - Marsh tests tracks live and goes back to tweak the ones that "fall flat" ([Stereofox](https://stereofox.com/interviews/interview-marsh)).
  - Durante kept the remix's chords only after road-testing them ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/)).

### Publication [P]

- **Breakdowns** ([Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/the-breakdown/what-is-the-recipe-for-an-effective-breakdown/)). This analysis uses house examples, not Anjunadeep tracks.
  - Breakdowns run 24–32 bars, built from 8-bar blocks.
  - Kick and bass leave, and the bass loses its lows.
  - Arp and chords start with their highs cut and open up across the section.
  - The pad gets heavy reverb in the last 8 bars.
  - A riser runs with a low-cut sweep into the drop, and the low end returns on the downbeat.
  - One example cuts everything 2 beats before the drop.
- **Build-ups.** Adding an element every 8 or 16 bars is the classic dance arrangement method (same source family; [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/the-breakdown/what-is-the-recipe-for-an-effective-breakdown/)).
- **Section map [P-weak].** EDMProd's map uses 16-bar blocks:
  1. melodic intro;
  2. drums, bass and lead;
  3. counter-melody, percussion and pad;
  4. clap and high ambience;
  5. swells and delay;
  6. a 32-bar breakdown with a new melody;
  7. a 16-bar breakdown with bass;
  8. the drop returns, at about 128 bars ([EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-melodic-house/)).

### Inference [I]

- **Length.** Extended mixes run about 6–8 minutes at 120–124 BPM, which is about 180–240 bars.
- **DJ intro and outro.** Each is 16–32 bars, drum-led, and harmonically sparse, so beatmatched blends don't clash.
- **Phrasing.** Every change lands on an 8-bar boundary. Most land on a 16-bar boundary.
- **The drop versus the groove.** The drop is mostly a *return* rather than a new idea: kick, sub and full chords come back, often with the lead or vocal hook now on top. Energy steps are small, and a melodic track's drop is rarely a big level jump.
- **Energy curve.** Gentle rise → plateau → one main breakdown at about 50–60% of the track → peak → mirror-image decline.

### Vocals [A]

- Böhmer prefers recording singers in person and records many takes ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/the-last-5-or-10-per-cent-drove-me-crazy-at-one-point-i-had-about-130-mixdowns-ive-come-to-realise-that-the-perfect-mix-doesnt-exist-ben-bohmer-on-overcoming-perfectionism-in-production)).
- HANA sings "many many parts" and the pair pick favourites ([Dancing Astronaut](https://dancingastronaut.com/2021/05/durante-hana-share-inspirations-behind-new-anjunadeep-ep-celestia-qa/)).
- Durante uses parallel pedal distortion on vocals ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/)).

### Percussion

These figures come from Attack's "deep melodic progressive techno" beat, which is adjacent to the genre rather than an Anjunadeep track ([Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/deep-melodic-progressive-techno/)) [P]:

- **Tempo and swing:** 120–123 BPM with 10% swing, using 808 sources.
- **Kick:** short and tight, leaving room for the sub, with a 30 Hz high-pass.
- **Clap:** one syncopated hit every 2 bars and a double hit every 4 bars, with hall reverb.
- **Hats:** offbeat.
- **Shaker:** 16th notes with varied velocity, auto-panned.
- **Rim:** nudged early to avoid the kick.
- **Congas and toms:** tuned to the root.
- **Drum bus:** glue compression of 2–3 dB, then tape emulation.

EDMProd adds [P-weak] ([EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-melodic-house/)):

- three panned shaker layers plus a tambourine;
- a clap on beats 2 and 4;
- a 3/16 delay on the arp;
- a reversed, pitched vocal chop with shimmer reverb.

### Inference [I]

- **Bass.** The bass is rolling and sustained: long, low-passed saw or square notes that change with the kick. A separate sub is kept mono below about 100 Hz.
- **Pads.** Pads are wide and slowly filtered or LFO-moved. Plucks and arps are short-decay and delay-heavy, often doubled an octave apart.
- **Textures.** Field recordings and drones sit 20–30 dB under the lead.

## 4. Mixing and effects

### Artist-sourced [A]

- **Böhmer on the mixdown.**
  - Mixing was the hardest part. He made about 130 mixdowns and concluded "the perfect mix doesn't exist".
  - His main challenge is balancing clean and non-clean sound so a mix works on big stages and home speakers ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/the-last-5-or-10-per-cent-drove-me-crazy-at-one-point-i-had-about-130-mixdowns-ive-come-to-realise-that-the-perfect-mix-doesnt-exist-ben-bohmer-on-overcoming-perfectionism-in-production)).
- **Böhmer's masterclass**, as summarised by [WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-masterclass/):
  - He decides per track whether a part is mono or stereo.
  - EQ is central.
  - Master-bus processing is light.
  - Sidechain compression goes on drums, and delay goes on pads.
  - Processors named are RP-Distort and PuigTec EQ.
- **Durante's sidechain.**
  - He sidechains the bass to the kick with Ableton Glue Compressor for a smoother groove.
  - He warns that overly precise LFO-style ducking "can kill the groove".
  - Subtle parallel distortion makes his mixes stand out ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/)).
- **Marsh's monitoring.**
  - He does his final mixdown on Sennheiser HD 6XX headphones.
  - He uses Genelec monitors only as a rough club check.
  - He uses Sonarworks correction ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2020/11/how-it-was-made-marsh-lailonie/)).

### Platform specification

- Spotify normalises to −14 LUFS integrated and recommends true peak below −1 dBTP, or below −2 dBTP for masters louder than −14 LUFS. Louder masters are simply turned down ([Spotify for Artists](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)).

### Inference [I]

- **Sidechain depth.** Bass ducks about 3–6 dB and pads about 2–4 dB, with release timed to the beat at about 100–200 ms at 122 BPM. That is gentler than EDM pumping, in line with Durante's "smoother groove".
- **Space.** Use long plate or hall reverbs (3–8 s) on pads and plucks, high-passed at about 200–300 Hz. Use a dotted-8th or 3/16 delay on arps. Automate reverb and delay feedback to build tension.
- **Width.** Below about 120 Hz, everything is mono: kick, sub and bass. Pads and doubled chords go wide. The lead sits near centre.
- **Loudness.** Club-oriented masters likely land louder than −14 LUFS, around −9 to −7 LUFS integrated, with breakdowns 4–8 LU (short-term) below the peak sections. This needs measurement of released tracks before it is encoded.

## Per-artist notes

- **Ben Böhmer** (Anjunadeep, later Ninja Tune). He describes his sound as "Melodic, dramatic, melancholic but happy" ([EDM.com](https://edm.com/interviews/ben-bohmer-hot-air-balloon-performance-interview/)). His workflow is piano-first in Ableton, with Diva as his main synth. He builds call-and-response from a shared source sound and alters his hooks every 4 bars. He has also written outside the dance context ([MusicRadar](https://musicradar.com/music-tech/dj-software/the-last-5-or-10-per-cent-drove-me-crazy-at-one-point-i-had-about-130-mixdowns-ive-come-to-realise-that-the-perfect-mix-doesnt-exist-ben-bohmer-on-overcoming-perfectionism-in-production), [WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/), [Stereofox](https://www.stereofox.com/interviews/interview-ben-bohmer/)).
- **Marsh** (Anjunadeep, now his own Marshan Records). His influences are trance, 909 drums and "jacking house grooves", and he aims for an "analog, rough around the edges" feel ([Stereofox](https://stereofox.com/interviews/interview-marsh)). He works in Logic with Sylenth1, Roland software synths, Diva and Alchemy, plus field recordings ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2020/11/how-it-was-made-marsh-lailonie/)). He detail-polishes for months.
- **Durante** (Anjunadeep). His influences are Underworld-style 90s progressive and analog hardware. He writes drums first and tunes percussion to key. He uses gentle bass sidechain, parallel distortion and arrangement as "conversation" ([Magnetic](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/), [DJ Life](https://djlifemag.com/2024/03/durante-enter-the-debut-interview/)).
- **Romain Garcia** (Anjunadeep). He is a Parisian former visual-art teacher who describes sound in terms of "colour, emotion, and narrative". His debut album *EURÊKA!* was made in a home studio in Biscarrosse, and he collaborated with Böhmer on "Cappadocia" ([Anjunadeep artist page](https://anjunadeep.com/gb/artists/196565-romain-garcia)). No first-party gear or technique source was found.
- **Nox Vahn** (Anjunadeep; formerly Fairchild). Bios describe him as classically trained and his style as "dark progressive" with "haunting" vocals ([EarMilk](https://earmilk.com/2019/07/17/nox-vahn-goes-against-the-current-in-his-latest-deep-diving-anjunadeep-production/)) [P]. Equipboard lists KRK Rokit 8 monitors from a photo ([Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/pros/nox-vahn)) [P-weak]. No first-party production interview was found.

## Rules of thumb for the fixture and checklist

Tags show the evidence behind each rule. [I] targets are starting points to verify by measuring released tracks.

1. **Tempo** is 120–124 BPM, defaulting to 122. Swing is 0–10% on 16th-note percussion. [P] [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/deep-melodic-progressive-techno/) and [I]
2. **Key** is minor (Aeolian or Dorian), with at least one major-leaning lift chord per loop. [I]
3. **Harmonic rhythm** is one chord per 1–2 bars, looping every 4 or 8 bars. At least half the chords are extended (7th, 9th or sus). [I], consistent with Böhmer's jazz voicing remark [A]
4. **The lead hook** repeats in 4-bar phrases, and each 4-bar repeat changes 1–3 notes. [A] [WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)
5. **The response line** sounds only in the lead's rests, with no overlap of more than 1 beat. It derives from the lead's own patch or audio through reprocessing. [A] (same source)
6. **The arp** is introduced in the intro (bar 1–17) and persists as the rhythmic spine. It uses a 3/16 or dotted-8th delay. [A] and [P-weak] [EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-melodic-house/)
7. **Sections** are multiples of 8 bars, with the main blocks at 16. The track totals 192–256 bars (about 6–8 min at 122 BPM). [P] [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/the-breakdown/what-is-the-recipe-for-an-effective-breakdown/) and [I]
8. **Intro and outro** are each 16–32 bars. They are drum-led with no full chord stack in the first 16 bars, and they mirror each other. [A] for the mirroring ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-masterclass/)), [I] for the bar counts
9. **Layer entry** adds or removes at most one major element per 8 bars. [P] [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/the-breakdown/what-is-the-recipe-for-an-effective-breakdown/)
10. **The main breakdown** is 24–32 bars and starts at 45–60% of the track. Kick and sub are absent. Filters open across it, and a riser plus a low-cut sweep fills its last 4–8 bars. [P] (same) and [I] for the position
11. **The drop** returns kick and sub on the downbeat, optionally after 2 beats of silence. Its short-term loudness stays within 1–2 LU of the pre-breakdown groove, because the drop is a return rather than a jump. [P] and [I]
12. **Bass** is a low-passed saw (or square), with a mono sub below 100 Hz (sine or low-passed saw). Notes change on kick hits. [A] [Durante](https://magneticmag.com/2022/07/how-it-was-made-booka-shade-fire-and-rain-durante-remix/) and [P-weak]
13. **Sidechain** ducks bass 3–6 dB and pads 2–4 dB from the kick, with release at 100–200 ms using a compressor, not a hard LFO shape. [A] for the method, [I] for the numbers
14. **The kick** is short with a tonal tail, high-passed at about 30 Hz. The sub is synthesised separately or layered under the kick's attack. [P] [Attack](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/deep-melodic-progressive-techno/) and [A] [Marsh](https://magneticmag.com/2020/11/how-it-was-made-marsh-lailonie/)
15. **Percussion** has closed hats on offbeats and a clap on 2 and 4 (or syncopated every 2 bars), with 16th-note shakers auto-panned. All tonal percussion is tuned to the root. [P] and [A] (Durante)
16. **Timbre** comes from analog-modelled sources: Diva- or Sylenth-class subtractive synths, unison or doubled oscillators panned left and right on chords, and slow LFO on pad filters. [A]
17. **Texture** includes one sustained drone or field-recording bed through most of the track, at least 20 dB below the lead. [A] for the drone ([Stereofox](https://www.stereofox.com/interviews/interview-ben-bohmer/)), [I] for the level
18. **Tension** comes from automation: delay feedback, filter cutoff and reverb send rise over 8–16 bars before section changes. [A] [WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-breaks-down-how-he-made-beyond-beliefs/)
19. **Saturation** is subtle and parallel on vocals, leads or bus (about 10–30% wet). The drum bus gets 2–3 dB glue compression. [A] (Durante) and [P] (Attack). The wet range is [I]
20. **Width** is mono below 120 Hz. Pads and chords are wide, and lead and vocal are centred. [I], with Böhmer's per-track mono or stereo decisions as [A]
21. **Master** loudness is about −9 to −7 LUFS integrated for club use, with true peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Breakdowns sit 4–8 LU below peak sections, and master processing is light. [A] for light master processing ([WeRaveYou](https://weraveyou.com/tech/ben-bohmer-masterclass/)), [I] for the numbers, and the platform reference is [Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/us/artists/article/loudness-normalization/)
